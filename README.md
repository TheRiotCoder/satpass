# SatPass

**▶ Open the app: <https://satpass-3wj.pages.dev>** (Cloudflare Pages, its own origin – biometric unlock works there). The old GitHub Pages copy `https://theriotcoder.github.io/satpass/` is a **deprecated mirror**: it keeps working (also offline) and shows a notice, but biometric unlock is not offered on it. Saved logins and favourites are stored per address and do **not** move: on the old copy use *Setup → Credentials → Encrypted backup → Export*, then *Import* on the new address.

Satellite and ISS pass predictor for ham radio operators (and, since 1.1.0, for other satellite types incl. all public **SAR** satellites), as an installable, offline-capable web app (PWA). It runs on Android, iPhone, Windows, Mac and Linux. It is plain HTML/CSS/vanilla JS with **no build step and no CDN at runtime**.

Predictions use public orbital elements (TLEs) from [CelesTrak](https://celestrak.org), propagated on your device with SGP4/SDP4. Your location never leaves the device.

> **Heads-up on accuracy:** predictions are only as good as the TLE behind them. A fresh LEO TLE is typically good to a km or two (a few seconds of pass timing), and it degrades every day. SatPass warns when element sets are older than 7 days. Frequencies in the Doppler tab are approximate (SAR radar frequencies are nominal and informational only). See [Caveats](#caveats).

## Features

- **3D Sky**: fourth tab on Live Tracker and Visible Now, using locally bundled [Three.js 0.180.0](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/tree/r180) under MIT ([license](js/vendor/three-LICENSE.txt), [provenance](js/vendor/three-README.md)). Stand at the active saved/custom observing point and drag to look around a perspective sky, including directly overhead. Scroll/pinch or use buttons to zoom; arrow keys turn, +/− zoom, Home faces north. Compass buttons, elevation grid, minimum-elevation ring and a live camera bearing help orient the view. Tap a marker to identify it; **Find selected** points the camera at a selected satellite or reference object. Uses the same satellite filters and Moon/planet/Polaris toggle as the chart. True-north bearings and geometric horizon; a decorative tree silhouette separates sky and ground in every direction. Trees, symbols and sky colors are illustrative; actual terrain, cloud, other stars, constellations and device orientation tracking are not modeled. Requires WebGL 2; if unavailable, the Sky View chart remains available. Rendering pauses off-tab/when hidden and releases GPU resources on leaving the page. Both Three.js modules are precached for offline use.

- **Location**: GPS button, manual lat/lon/elevation, **Maidenhead grid** entry and display (type a grid to get lat/lon, or lat/lon to get the grid; 4/6/8/10 characters). Saved in `localStorage`. Local-time / UTC toggle in the header.
- **Satellite list** with search and favorites. Defaults to the **ISS (25544)**. The curated set covers ISS, Tiangong (CSS), SO-50, AO-91, AO-92, AO-7, RS-44, FO-29, AO-73, CAS-4A/4B, IO-117, TEVEL 1–9, NOAA 15/18/19 and Meteor-M2 3/4. All ~185 bundled satellites are searchable.
- **Categories** (Sats tab chips with counts): **Amateur**, **Stations**, **Weather**, **SAR**, **Earth observation**, **Navigation** (GPS, Galileo, GLONASS, BeiDou) and **Science**, plus Favourites (across all categories) and name / NORAD / category search. Only Amateur, Stations and Weather load at start; the others are fetched the first time you open them (and cached). Starlink, OneWeb and geostationary satellites are not listed; a single Starlink/OneWeb can be looked up by name + number (e.g. `STARLINK-1234`).
- **SAR satellites** ([list below](#sar-satellites-radar-panel)): the Doppler tab shows a **Radar panel** (band, nominal centre frequency, bandwidth, polarizations, modes, source) instead of up/downlinks, with the one-way carrier Doppler at your position, ascending/descending direction, local solar time and an approximate swath-proximity check at closest approach.
- **Pass prediction** for 1–10 days: AOS / max-elevation (TCA) / LOS times, azimuth + compass point, duration, max elevation, sorted list, tap a pass for detail. Minimum-elevation filter (default 10°; AOS/LOS are when the satellite crosses that elevation). **VISIBLE** flag for ISS/Tiangong: satellite sunlit (Earth shadow test), observer's sun elevation below −6°, satellite above horizon.
- **Pass detail**: polar sky plot (AOS/TCA/LOS marks, time ticks, visible part highlighted, live "NOW" marker), az/el/range/range-rate table at 10/20/30 s, `.ics` export, in-app reminder.
- **Live tracker**: az, el, range, range-rate, lat/lon/alt, speed, sunlit/eclipsed, 1 s updates; separate **2D Map / 3D Globe / Sky View / 3D Sky** tabs (choice remembered), an offline globe with drag/touch/keyboard rotation, zoom and a center-on-satellite control, satellite altitude and Earth-fixed trajectories; equirectangular map with a small bundled coastline, ground track (past + future), footprint circle (0° horizon and your minimum elevation). Sky View shows the selected satellite from your saved observing location with live bearing/elevation/range and lighting conditions; below-horizon satellites are explained without plotting them above the horizon.
- **Visible Now**: all catalog satellites above the observing point, updated every second, with **2D Map / 3D Globe / Sky View / 3D Sky** tabs (globe heights compressed for readability, with an actual-scale option). Uses your saved location or a separate custom latitude/longitude/elevation. Shows true-north compass bearings, elevation, range, sunlight/sky conditions and TLE age; filter by minimum elevation, compass direction, or sunlit + dark sky. Sky View is a whole-sky chart looking up: north at top, east on the left, overhead at center and horizon at rim, with 30°/60° elevation rings. Tap a sky marker or select a row for the satellite name and current readouts; labels avoid overlapping when enabled. Stars/constellations and phone orientation tracking are not included. Select a row to label the satellite, or open its Live page. Loads all bundled catalog categories, including imports/lookups; this is not a catalog of every orbiting object and favorable lighting does not guarantee naked-eye visibility.
- **Doppler calculator** with a bundled, *approximate* frequency table (FM repeaters, linear transponders, APRS/digital, APT/LRPT): live Doppler-corrected receive/transmit frequency, a passband slider for linear transponders, and a Doppler-vs-time table across any upcoming pass.
- **Alerts**: "Next pass" countdown banner (favorites); per-pass reminders with the Notification API where the platform allows it; `.ics` calendar export (with an alert) for one pass or the whole list.
- **Sky references**: both Sky Views include a shared **Show Moon, planets and Polaris** toggle (on by default). Above-horizon Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune and Polaris appear as labeled reference markers, with bearing/elevation readouts and the Moon’s illuminated percentage. Satellite filters/counts do not apply to these references; daylight and brightness may prevent seeing them, and some planets need optical aid. Calculations use [Astronomy Engine 2.1.19](https://github.com/cosinekitty/astronomy/tree/v2.1.19), MIT ([bundled license](js/vendor/astronomy-LICENSE.md), [source/provenance](js/vendor/astronomy-README.md)), with observer parallax and geometric elevations. Polaris is a lavender star labeled “Polaris · North Star”, with its own bearing/elevation readout. No runtime service or API key is needed.
- **Offline**: app shell, bundled TLE snapshots for **every category** and the map are precached. First launch offline works from the snapshots; cached TLEs are used afterwards.

## Install

**iPhone / iPad (Safari)**
1. Open the app URL in **Safari** (not an in-app browser).
2. Tap **Share** (square with the up arrow).
3. Tap **Add to Home Screen**, then **Add**.

**Android (Chrome)**
1. Open the app URL in **Chrome**.
2. Tap **Install** on the in-app banner, or **⋮ → Install app** (or **Add to Home screen**).

**Desktop (Chrome / Edge)**: click the install icon in the address bar.

After the first visit it works without a connection.

### Notifications on iPhone / Android

- **Android / desktop Chrome**: tap "Remind me" on a pass and allow notifications. Reminders fire while the app is open or still running in the background.
- **iPhone**: web notifications only exist for apps **added to the Home Screen** (iOS 16.4+). In plain Safari the button explains this and the in-app banner/toast is used instead.
- **Limitation (all platforms)**: a web app that is fully closed cannot be woken at a scheduled time without a push server, and SatPass has none (nothing is sent off-device). For a dependable alarm use **Add to calendar (.ics)**; the exported events include an alert. The in-app **Next pass** banner always works while the app is open.

## Data sources and attribution

- **Orbital elements**: [CelesTrak](https://celestrak.org) GP data (Dr. T.S. Kelso) via `https://celestrak.org/NORAD/elements/gp.php`. Groups used: `amateur`, `stations`, `weather` (+ `CATNR=25338/28654/33591` for NOAA 15/18/19, which aren't in a group), `resource` (Earth observation), `gps-ops`, `galileo`, `glo-ops`, `beidou` (Navigation) and `science`. **SAR** has no CelesTrak group (`GROUP=radar` is *radar calibration spheres*, not SAR satellites), so it is a curated list queried by name (`NAME=SENTINEL-1`, …). Satellites numbered ≥ 100000 (e.g. newer ICEYE) exist only in CelesTrak's JSON/OMM format; SatPass converts them to Alpha-5 TLEs (`A0889` for 100889). CelesTrak serves `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`, so the browser can fetch it directly. SatPass is not affiliated with or endorsed by CelesTrak.
- **Rate courtesy**: CelesTrak asks clients not to re-download the same data more than about once every 2 hours. SatPass refreshes the three core categories automatically at most every **6 h**; the other categories are fetched **lazily** (first time you open them, otherwise the bundled snapshot is shown) and then at most once per **24 h** automatically; the manual button is allowed at most every **2 h** per category, requests are spaced ≥ 1.2 s apart, failures back off 30 min, and a network error or HTTP 403/429 stops the batch immediately. Name lookups are only for specific text, capped at 40 results, and never for whole mega-constellations.
- **Fallbacks**: if CelesTrak can't be reached (offline, blocked by a network or content blocker) SatPass keeps the last cached/bundled TLEs and says so, tries your other enabled sources (see *Data sources* below) and always offers **file import / pasting TLEs** on the Sats tab.
- **Bundled snapshots** (`data/tle-snapshot.js` = Amateur/Stations/Weather, loaded at start; `data/snap/{sar,eo,nav,science}.js` loaded on demand and precached by the service worker; each with its fetch time): used on first launch and offline. Regenerate everything with `python3 tools/update_snapshot.py` at release time (it caches responses and waits ≥ 2 h before re-requesting a query).
- **Coastline** (`data/world.js`): simplified from Natural Earth 110m land (public domain), generated by `tools/make_world.py`.
- **Frequencies** (`js/radio.js`): compiled from public amateur-satellite information; approximate and out of date quickly. Verify with [AMSAT's status page](https://www.amsat.org/status/) before operating.
- **SAR radar parameters** (`data/sar.js`): taken from agency / operator documentation first (ESA SentiWiki, Canadian Space Agency, JAXA EORC, NASA/JPL NISAR, ISRO/NRSC, ICEYE, Capella, Synspective, iQPS, …), then [ESA eoPortal](https://www.eoportal.org) and the [WMO OSCAR](https://space.oscar.wmo.int) satellite database (WMO / OSCAR is acknowledged as a source; SatPass is not affiliated with either). Each mission lists its sources; NORAD ids were checked against CelesTrak.

## Data sources: CelesTrak, Space-Track, SatNOGS, N2YO (1.2.0, hardened in 1.2.1)

CelesTrak stays the default and is read straight from your browser. If it is unreachable for you, SatPass keeps working from the cache / bundled snapshot and can refresh from the other sources you enable in **Setup → Data sources** (order and on/off per source; status line per source with the last success and last problem; *Test connection* per source; *Forget credentials*). When the preferred source fails (network / blocked / 403 / 429 / bad credentials) the next enabled one is tried automatically and the Sats tab says so, e.g. "CelesTrak unreachable — using Space-Track.". If **every** source fails, the message points to the manual path: **Import a TLE / OMM file** (TLE/3LE text or OMM JSON) or paste TLEs on the Sats tab.

**The CORS reality (checked October 2026 from the real `https://theriotcoder.github.io` origin in headless Chrome):** only CelesTrak sends `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`. `www.space-track.org` (login and queries), `db.satnogs.org` (`/api/tle/`, `/api/transmitters/`, also with an `Authorization` header) and `api.n2yo.com` send **no** CORS headers, so a static page cannot read their responses. There is no way around that in a backend-less PWA. SatPass therefore ships an optional, tiny **CORS proxy (Cloudflare Worker) in [`proxy/`](proxy/README.md) that you deploy to your own free Cloudflare account**; you put its URL into Setup (only `https://*.workers.dev` and `http://localhost` are allowed by the page's Content-Security-Policy; edit `index.html` to allow another host). Without a proxy those three sources show "not set up" and are skipped; CelesTrak and file import keep working.

| Source | How it is called | Auth | Rate limits (documented) and what SatPass does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **CelesTrak** | direct `gp.php` GET | none | asks clients not to repeat a query within ~2 h; SatPass: automatic core refresh ≤ every 6 h, manual ≥ 2 h apart, 1.2 s between requests, stops on 403/429/network |
| **Space-Track** | via proxy: `POST /ajaxauth/login` (session cookie `chocolatechip`) then `GET /basicspacedata/query/class/gp/NORAD_CAT_ID/…/format/json`, then logout | account user name (e-mail) + password, sent in headers to *your* proxy (or held there as Worker secrets) | < 30 requests/min and < 300/h; the same GP query at most **once an hour**; SatPass batches 150 NORAD ids per request, 2 s apart, caps itself at 25/min and 240/h, and never repeats the same pull within an hour |
| **SatNOGS DB** | via proxy: `GET /api/tle/` (the multi-id filter only honours one id, so the bulk list is fetched once and filtered) and `/api/transmitters/?satellite__norad_cat_id=` | optional `Authorization: Token …` | 60/h anonymous, 240/h with a token (SatNOGS staff statement; DB limits are not published); SatPass caps itself at 40/h |
| **N2YO** | via proxy: `/tle/{id}`, `/radiopasses/…` | API key (`apiKey` query parameter upstream – the proxy appends it, the browser never puts it in a URL) | free key per hour: tle 1000, positions 1000, visual/radio passes 100, above 100; errors come back as HTTP 200 + `{"error":…}`; SatPass caps itself at 900 and 90, refreshes at most 40 satellites per pull (favourites first) |

Space-Track also publishes new GP data roughly hourly; one pull per hour per query is the rule. Satellites with NORAD ids ≥ 100000 (Alpha-5) work via Space-Track JSON; SatNOGS and N2YO have no such objects.

**SatNOGS live transmitters (optional).** *Fetch live transmitters from SatNOGS* adds active, alive transmitters from SatNOGS DB to the Doppler tab as separate choices labelled "SatNOGS DB – live, fetched <date>". The bundled nominal table stays the default and is never silently replaced; the Setup card shows a comparison (bundled entry vs nearest live transmitter).

**Compare with N2YO.** On a pass detail page (when N2YO is set up) the button asks N2YO's `/radiopasses` (0° horizon crossings, one transaction, answer cached for 30 min) and shows N2YO's AOS / max / LOS next to SatPass's with the deltas, plus the remaining-quota information N2YO reports (`transactionscount`, transactions in the last 60 minutes).

**Password managers, biometrics, auto-lock and backups (1.3.0).**
- **Recommended route (most secure): the proxy with Worker secrets.** Setup → Data sources lists it first (section 1). Put your Space-Track login in the Worker's secrets, enter only the proxy URL and the *proxy access token* in SatPass – your Space-Track password never enters the browser.
- **Password-manager friendly fields.** Every credential field is a real `<form method="post">` field with a `name` and a proper `autocomplete` (`username` + `current-password` for the Space-Track login, `current-password` for tokens/keys with a hidden account label such as "SatNOGS API token (SatPass)", `new-password` for the vault passphrase and backup passphrase; never `autocomplete="off"`; the unlock field uses `current-password`). *Test connection* and *Save proxy settings* are submit buttons whose handlers call `preventDefault()` (no navigation; `method="post"` + `form-action 'none'` in the CSP mean a script failure could never put a secret into a URL). After a **successful** test the card re-renders (the form disappears), which is the signal managers such as Chrome/iCloud Passwords use to offer saving; a failed test keeps the form so a wrong password is not offered. Managers can also save/fill manually. Help text in the app: *Save in your password manager; SatPass can store an encrypted copy on this device.*
- **Unlock with biometrics (optional; not offered on `*.github.io`).** Setup → Credentials on this device → *Enable biometric unlock* (needs an encrypted vault, i.e. a passphrase). SatPass creates a platform **passkey** (Face ID / Touch ID / Android fingerprint / Windows Hello; `userVerification: "required"`, non-discoverable `residentKey: "discouraged"`, `rpId` = this page's host) and asks it for a **WebAuthn PRF** secret. That 32-byte secret goes through HKDF-SHA256 to an AES-GCM key that wraps the vault's random **data key** (padded to a fixed size so the length reveals nothing; record bound to rpId/credential id/salt as additional data). The **passphrase is never wrapped or stored**: it only protects the same data key through PBKDF2, so a biometric record can never reveal it. Unlocking asks for the biometric, unwraps the data key in memory and opens the vault; the **passphrase always remains the fallback and the recovery path** (wrong finger, deleted passkey, new phone, or no PRF just means: type the passphrase). **Changing the passphrase rotates the data key** (new random key, credentials re-encrypted and wrapped by the new passphrase, so an old copy of the vault plus the old passphrase opens nothing saved afterwards). The biometric record wrapped the old key and the PRF secret is never kept, so it is **switched off by a passphrase change and the app tells you**: your passphrase works at once, and you turn biometrics on again with one tap (nothing is lost; this is the safe choice because re-wrapping would need the biometric secret). Removing the passphrase or *Forget credentials* also deletes the biometric record. *Reset* (About) keeps credentials, the biometric record and auto-lock settings (it says so), so it never orphans the passkey. The option is **hidden** where the browser cannot do it (no HTTPS, no WebAuthn, no platform authenticator, IP-address host, `getClientCapabilities` reports no PRF); PRF support itself is only proven by trying, and an authenticator without PRF is refused with a clear message and nothing is stored.
  - **Why it is off on github.io (shared origin).** `<user>.github.io` is **one browser origin for every project site of the account** (a new repo does not help): `/satpass/`, `/rf-toolbox/`, `/hamlog/` … share `localStorage` and a passkey's `rpId` cannot be narrower than the host. A script in any sibling app could read the wrapped record from the shared storage and ask the same passkey for the PRF output after a single biometric tap. So SatPass does not offer biometric unlock on any `github.io` host and **deletes any `satpass:bio` record on load there** (1.3.0/1.3.1 records – which also wrapped the passphrase itself – are deleted everywhere on first load of 1.3.2; set it up again where it is available). The passphrase vault, auto-lock and backups work everywhere. Biometrics stay available on `localhost` and on a host that serves **only SatPass**. SatPass can only detect `github.io`; it **cannot see what else lives on your host**, so enabling it on any other address asks you to confirm that SatPass is the only app there.
  - **Hosting SatPass on a dedicated host (to get biometrics).** **A GitHub Pages custom domain does NOT help by itself:** a custom domain on your user site (`<user>.github.io`) serves *every* project repo of the account (`/satpass/`, `/rf-toolbox/`, `/hamlog/` …) from that one domain, i.e. one shared origin again. You need a host where SatPass is the only app: (a) **your own Netlify site via Netlify Drop** – <https://app.netlify.com/drop>, drag the repo folder in (or connect `TheRiotCoder/satpass`, no build command, publish directory `/`), you get `https://<name>.netlify.app`; (b) **Cloudflare Pages** – same, one project for SatPass only; (c) a **domain or subdomain used only by SatPass** (e.g. `satpass.example.com`), served by a static host – with GitHub Pages this is only OK if that domain belongs to a *separate* Pages site/repo whose other apps are not served under the same host (e.g. other apps stay on other hosts/subdomains; project sites that inherit the user-site custom domain share it). Then add the new origin to `ALLOWED_ORIGINS` of your proxy Worker (`proxy/wrangler.toml`, exact origin, comma separated), redeploy it, and open SatPass there (favourites and credentials are per origin – use *Encrypted backup* to move credentials). HTTPS is required (localhost is fine for testing).
  - *Verified vs not.* Wrap/unwrap, padding, HKDF/AES-GCM binding, the data-key vault, legacy-blob migration, request shape, Safari-style "PRF only from `get()`", error mapping: unit-tested with a stubbed authenticator (`tests/biometric.test.js`, `tests/sources.test.js`, `tests/sources-store.test.js`). Full enrol → lock → biometric unlock → failed verification → removed passkey → no-PRF authenticator flows run end-to-end against **Chrome's CDP virtual authenticator with PRF on `localhost`** (`tests/browser-vault.js`); on the live Pages host the test asserts that biometrics are off and records are purged. **Not tested on a real phone or sensor** – treat real Face ID / fingerprint behaviour as untested.
- **Auto-lock.** An encrypted vault locks after a configurable idle time (default 15 min) and when the page has been hidden/backgrounded for a configurable time (default 5 min); both can be set to *Never*. *Lock now* does it immediately. Locking drops the decrypted credentials *and* the passphrase from memory, re-renders the fields empty and disabled, and automatic refreshes skip login-requiring sources until you unlock. (JavaScript cannot overwrite strings in place: they are dropped and left to the garbage collector.) Credentials without a passphrase (tab-only) are **not auto-locked and not cleared on idle**: locking them would destroy them, so they stay in memory/`sessionStorage` until the tab closes (use a passphrase if you want them to lock).
- **Encrypted backup.** *Export encrypted backup* writes `satpass-credentials-YYYY-MM-DD.satpass-creds.json`: your credentials and proxy URL encrypted with a separate backup passphrase (≥ 10 characters, PBKDF2-SHA256 600 000 → AES-GCM). It contains no plaintext. *Import* decrypts it (wrong passphrase / junk / tampered iteration counts / an edited file header are rejected cleanly, size-capped; backups from 1.3.0/1.3.1 still import), then **asks for confirmation before it replaces any credential you already have or changes the proxy URL** – the question names the new proxy host, because a crafted backup could otherwise repoint your logins to someone else's Worker. It merges the values into the current vault (encrypted if you have a passphrase, tab-only otherwise). Both are refused while locked. The file header (app, version, date, note) is bound to the ciphertext as AES-GCM additional data.

**Export SAR list (1.3.1).** On the Sats tab choose the *SAR radar* category: **⬇ CSV** (UTF-8 with BOM, opens correctly in Excel; LF line ends, RFC-4180 quoting), **⬇ JSON** (`{columns, rows}` with real numbers / `null`s / arrays), **⧉ Copy (spreadsheet)** (tab-separated, pastes into Sheets/Excel) and **⧉ Copy CSV**. One row per satellite from the bundled `data/sar.js` (works offline): `mission, operator, band, centerFreqMHz, bandwidthMHz, approx, polarizations, modes, swathKm, swathMode, secondaryBands, noradId, satName, satNote, notes, sources`. A value that is not verified in the data stays blank (never guessed); `approx` is `yes` for approximate values; `secondaryBands` is compact JSON; missions without a public TLE get a row with blank `noradId` and `satNote` = "no public TLE" (a mission with no satellites listed gets one row with blank satellite columns); `sources` are space-separated URLs. The CSV is byte-identical to the reference file in `tests/fixtures/satpass-sar-stats.csv`. Values are nominal and informational, not for reception.

**Credential handling and privacy.**
- **Where credentials live (1.2.1).** Nothing secret is saved to `localStorage` in plaintext: without a passphrase the Space-Track user name/password, SatNOGS token, N2YO key and proxy access token are kept in memory + this tab's `sessionStorage` only (gone when the tab closes; a browser's session-restore feature may still persist `sessionStorage` to disk, so this is "not saved to local storage", not a guarantee about the disk); with a passphrase (≥ 10 characters) they are stored as a PBKDF2-SHA256 (600 000 iterations) → AES-GCM blob and you unlock them each session. Reason: GitHub Pages serves every project of an account from one origin, so any script of another project on `<user>.github.io` could read plaintext `localStorage`. Blobs from 1.2.0 (250 000 iterations) still decrypt; the stored iteration count is clamped to 100 000…2 000 000 so a tampered blob can neither hang the tab nor weaken the key derivation. A 1.2.0 plaintext copy is still read, flagged with a banner, and you can encrypt it or delete it (keep for this tab only). While locked, the fields and *Test connection* are disabled and `setCreds`/`lock`/`removePassphrase` refuse to touch the encrypted blob. **Safest:** keep the Space-Track password out of the browser entirely – deploy the proxy with Worker secrets and give SatPass only the proxy URL + access token.
- They are sent only to the proxy URL you entered, in request **headers** (never in URLs, never in a POST to anyone else), and the proxy forwards them only to the matching service. Recommended: store them as Worker secrets and give SatPass only the proxy URL + access token (see `proxy/README.md`).
- The service worker never touches these requests (all cross-origin requests, API hosts, any URL with a key-like query parameter and any request carrying a credential header bypass it, so nothing credentialed is cached); error messages shown or logged are redacted; credentials never appear in .ics exports; the page's Content-Security-Policy limits `connect-src` to `self`, CelesTrak, `*.workers.dev` and localhost.
- *Forget credentials* deletes every stored credential. *Reset* (About) keeps data-source settings and credentials on purpose. Wrong credentials stop automatic use of that source until you change them (Space-Track may lock accounts after repeated failures).
- SatPass has no server, no analytics and no accounts of its own.

**Read this before relying on the numbers.**
- Radar frequencies are **nominal** centre frequencies of the radar instrument (band names per IEEE: P 0.3–1, L 1–2, S 2–4, C 4–8, X 8–12 GHz). They are **informational only and not for reception**: SAR downlinks use other bands, individual satellites, modes and tuning may differ, and nothing here helps to receive or transmit.
- A value that could not be verified from a source is shown as **"frequency not verified"** (or "not verified") rather than guessed; values marked *approx.* are approximate. Bandwidth is the largest verified bandwidth; imaging modes use less.
- **One-way carrier Doppler** (shown in the radar panel) = −f·(range rate)/c at *your* position. It is labelled "one-way carrier Doppler at observer, informational; actual SAR processing uses two-way Doppler centroid with antenna pointing". It is not the Doppler centroid of a SAR image.
- **Ascending/descending** is the sign of the z-component of the satellite's inertial (TEME) velocity at closest approach (north-going = ascending); **local solar time** is the *mean* local solar time (UTC hour + longitude/15, no equation of time) of the sub-satellite point; **swath proximity** compares the distance from you to the ground track with a nominal swath width of a representative mode and is approximate (look angles, mode and pointing are not modelled).
- Sentinel-1B failed in December 2021 but CelesTrak still publishes elements for it; it is listed and marked *failed*. Sentinel-6 (a radar altimeter) is not SAR and is excluded. KOMPSAT-6 and TecSAR-1 are listed as **no public TLE** (not in CelesTrak's public data). NISAR also carries an S-band radar (shown as secondary).
- The catalogue is curated by hand and is a snapshot in time; missions launched later can be found by name/NORAD search (a new `ICEYE-X…` is recognised as SAR automatically).

## How it works / SGP4 choice

Propagation uses **[satellite.js](https://github.com/shashwatak/satellite-js) 6.0.2** (MIT; the UMD minified build is vendored in `js/vendor/` with its licence), which implements SGP4/SDP4 (near-earth and deep-space) per Vallado's reference code. Chosen over writing our own SGP4 (error-prone) and over satellite.js 7.x (ESM-only/WASM, no single-file build, which doesn't fit "no build step, works from any static host").

Everything else is our own code: TEME→ECEF rotation, WGS-84 observer geometry, az/el/range/range-rate, Meeus low-precision sun position (~0.01°), cylindrical Earth-shadow test, pass search (20 s sampling + bisection for AOS/LOS, golden-section for max elevation), Doppler, `.ics`, Maidenhead.

### Polaris reference

Polaris uses the bundled Astronomy Engine `Star1` definition with [SIMBAD’s alf UMi catalog entry](https://simbad.u-strasbg.fr/simbad/sim-basic?Ident=Polaris) (Hipparcos re-reduction, van Leeuwen 2007): ICRS epoch J2000 RA 02h31m49.09456s, declination +89°15′50.7923″, parallax 7.54 mas (about 433 light-years). Astronomy Engine converts this to the observing point’s current geometric bearing/elevation, including precession/nutation and observer corrections. The small stellar proper motion is omitted (about 1 arcsecond from 2000 to 2026, below chart resolution). Polaris is not pinned to exact north, and is hidden whenever below the active point’s horizon. No star catalog download is needed at runtime.

## Approximations (read this)

- **Geometric elevation.** Elevations, AOS and LOS use the geometric (airless) direction to the satellite. Atmospheric refraction (about 0.5° at the horizon, less higher up) is ignored, so a real signal can appear slightly before the predicted AOS and fade slightly after LOS. AOS/LOS are the times the satellite crosses your *minimum elevation* (default 10°), where refraction is only a few arc-minutes.
- **Cylindrical Earth shadow.** "Sunlit" uses a simple cylinder of radius 6378.137 km behind a spherical Earth. There is no penumbra, atmosphere or oblateness, so sunlit/eclipse transitions are good to a few seconds. The sun position is a low-precision (about 0.01°) formula.
- **−6° visibility rule.** A pass is flagged VISIBLE when the satellite is sunlit, above the horizon, and the sun is more than 6° below your horizon (civil twilight). That is a rule of thumb: brightness (size, range, phase angle), sky glow, weather and terrain also matter. Only bright objects (ISS, Tiangong) get the tag.

## How passes are found

`SatPasses.findPasses(sat, obs, startMs, endMs, {minEl, stepSec, maxOverrunMs})` scans with an adaptive step (10 s when the elevation is within ~20° of the threshold, up to 30 s when far below it, 20 s inside a pass) and detects local elevation maxima, so a pass that only just clears the threshold between two samples (a few seconds above it) is still found. AOS/LOS are bisected to 0.1 s. Window edges:

- **In progress at the start:** the pass is returned with its *true* AOS (found by looking back up to 24 h), `aosBeforeStart = true` (alias `inProgressAtStart`).
- **Still open at the end:** the pass is kept and LOS is searched past the window end until it really ends (bounded by `maxOverrunMs`, default 14 h, enough for AO-10-style passes); `losAfterWindow = true`.
- **LOS (or AOS) not found within the limit:** the pass is kept, the time is a lower bound and `truncated = true` (`aosTruncated` for the AOS side). The app shows "LOS ≥" on the list and a warning on the detail page.

## Caveats

- **TLE age/accuracy**: SGP4 + TLE error grows with age (ISS can lose several km/day after reboosts or manoeuvres). A pass can be minutes off with week-old elements. Always confirm important passes with a live source.
- **Visible passes** are geometric (sunlit satellite, dark sky, above the horizon). Brightness, weather, terrain, refraction and attitude are not modelled. The VISIBLE tag is only applied to bright objects (ISS, Tiangong).
- **Doppler** is first-order (non-relativistic); frequencies, tones and satellite status change and some satellites are off for long periods. Approximate by design.
- **AO-92, CAS-4A and CAS-4B** are in the catalogue/frequency table but have **no current element set in CelesTrak's public data** at the time of the snapshot (retired/decayed/unpublished); paste TLEs if you have them. NOAA 15/18/19 APT was being retired; check they're still transmitting.
- **SAR radar frequencies** are nominal and informational (see [SAR satellites](#sar-satellites-radar-panel)); several are marked "frequency not verified" (Capella, LuTan-1, KOMPSAT-6, TecSAR-1) and HJ-2E/F and ASNARO-2 are approximate. NovaSAR-1's 3.2 GHz is the nominal centre of its 3.1–3.3 GHz S-band allocation; iQPS quotes 9.6 GHz for QPS-SAR while WMO OSCAR lists 9.38 GHz.
- **Starlink / OneWeb / other mega-constellations** are not in any category (thousands of satellites); look up a single satellite by name + number. **Geostationary** satellites are skipped by the pass finder (they don't "pass").
- **Notifications**: see above. iOS requires a Home Screen install, and nothing can fire while the app is fully closed.
- **Privacy**: location and settings live only in this browser's `localStorage`. By default the only network traffic is the CelesTrak GET requests; if you enable the optional sources, requests go to the proxy you configured (see *Data sources*). Your location is never sent to a data source (except an approximate 0.01° latitude/longitude when you press *Compare with N2YO*, which N2YO needs to compute passes).
- Developed and tested in desktop headless Chrome and node; not yet exercised on physical Android/iOS devices.
